### 1. Money
Income comes from farming, taxes, trade, mines and merchants. Taxes draw gold by population and tax rate; mines pay a fixed sum per turn; trade comes from road and port links. The main expenses are unit upkeep, paid every turn for every fielded unit, and wages for generals and agents. A city has free-upkeep slots by wall level (palisade 2, wooden 3, stone 4, large stone 5, huge stone 6). A slot holds one unit the game marks free: a militia type that city can recruit now (Town, Spear, Archer or the author Militia and the like; Peasants are not free). It costs 0 instead of its upkeep (Town Militia 125 a turn) while it stays in that city. Castles have no free slots, so a militia moved to a castle pays full upkeep. Militia are garrison troops: they keep order cheaply and defend a settlement under attack or siege; they are not a field army. Unspent gold earns nothing: spend surplus on economy and order, then armies.

### 2. Agents
Agents are recruited in settlements with the prerequisite building (market, inn, church, council chamber).
* **Spy**: scouts armies and settlements, infiltrates walls, may open a besieged settlement's gates; risks execution on failure.
* **Assassin**: kills enemy agents and generals, or sabotages buildings; risks capture and execution.
* **Diplomat**: negotiates treaties, trade rights and bribes; a failed bribe loses only the attempt.
* **Merchant**: stands on a resource tile for gold each turn; rival merchants can buy him out.
* **Priest**: converts a region to your religion and raises his piety; high piety can make a cardinal, low piety a heretic.
* **Princess**: acts as a diplomat; accepts suitors the game offers (they join your family as generals) or marries a foreign general to bind an alliance; her charm fades if she stays unmarried.

### 3. The Church
Catholic factions manage Papal Standing: churches, converted provinces and the Pope's missions (some ask for gold) raise it. Attacking Catholic factions after a papal warning lowers it and leads to excommunication: severe public order penalties everywhere, and Catholic neighbours and Crusades may attack you. At high standing you can call a Crusade against excommunicated Catholics, rebels or non-Christian cities; crusaders have cheap upkeep and move faster, and a successful Crusade pays 1,000-2,000 florins plus a chevron per unit. It crosses water by ship: lose the fleet and you lose the army.

### 4. Sieges
A settlement with no walls can be stormed at once. Against walls the besiegers build rams, ladders or towers (a big army builds rams or ladders on the first turn and can storm then; towers and several engines take longer). Artillery (ballista, catapult, trebuchet; bombard and cannon after gunpowder) breaches walls without engines, so an army that brings it can storm the turn it arrives. It moves 20% slower, costs 370-870 plus 150-250 upkeep a turn, and comes from a siege works (ballista, then catapult, then trebuchet by upgrade) or a gunsmith; pools hold 3. Worth it for a campaign, not one town. Or hold the blockade until the holdout counter reaches zero and the defenders starve into surrender. Besieged garrisons suffer attrition and may sally. A spy inside the settlement has a chance each turn to open the gates, allowing an immediate assault.

### 5. Armies
In auto-resolved battles the general's Command stars weigh heavily beside troop quality and numbers. Judge an army by units, men, quality and the general's stars. The strength rating is the game's combat score, not a head count: never quote it as troops. Damaged units fight worse; merge them, or bring them into a settlement with the right military building to retrain to full size. Recruitment is limited by local pools that refill fractionally over turns.
Reinforcements: when an army fights or besieges, friendly armies next to it (allies too) join the battle and count in the odds; the radius isn't documented. To bring two stacks, park the second beside the target one turn, attack the next. An army holds 20 units; reinforcements enter as slots open.
War runs on money: a rich realm keeps big armies paid and takes towns fast; early on build income. Scout by sea: a spare ship or two shows an invasion fleet before it lands. A fleet full of soldiers is easy prey at sea: one big fleet stack sinks it and the army aboard with it, and you never fight the army.

### 6. Order and Growth
Public order rises with garrisons, law buildings, low taxes and a governor's Chivalry, Dread or Piety. It falls with high taxes, squalor (big population), religious unrest, devastation and distance from the capital. Aim for 80%+. Below about 70% citizens riot, damaging buildings and killing troops, and long enough riots end in revolt: the settlement and its garrison go over to the rebels.

### 7. Diplomacy
Reputation measures how reliably you keep agreements. Attacking allies, breaking treaties or executing prisoners lowers it, so factions accept fewer deals and attack more. Trade rights cost nothing and raise port and road income for both once borders touch or ports connect. Alliances deter attacks, but AI factions break them when your borders are weak.

### 8. Family
Family members serve as generals and governors. Governors pick up traits from how their city is run (high taxes and idleness breed bad ones); battles and building give good ones. New family come only from sons coming of age, marrying off princesses, and adopting "Man of the Hour" captains or suitors. The game names the heir from the adult men of the family.

### 9. Rules of thumb
- Never leave a settlement with no garrison while an enemy or rebel army is within a few tiles: thin garrisons fall to small forces. Make a field army from spare units, never by stripping the homeland.
- Before raising a tax, read the settlement's public order. Near 70% don't tax higher; a thin garrison plus a tax hike is how settlements revolt.
- Keep new conquests on low tax with a garrison until order passes 80%. In unhappy cities build law and order buildings before extra income.
- Debt is a tool: going negative to take a town is often right. In debt, gold orders are refused, so recruit and build first. Disband only units that serve no goal.
- Overextension (falling income, rioting distant cities, garrisons needed everywhere): consolidate before the next push.
- Cities earn, castles train troops: castles on the borders, cities inside.
- Refill a garrison with free militia up to the city's slots before paying for other garrison units. Castles have their own law buildings.
- Starve a walled town unless you have artillery or far more men than its garrison; storming walls with too few men loses the army.
- Don't break a treaty or attack an ally: reputation drives every faction's willingness to deal. Keep garrisons on allied borders.
- The clock: 2 years a turn, 225 turns (1080-1530). Mongols arrive about T64-80, gunpowder T105-110, Black Death T133, Timurids T144-156.
